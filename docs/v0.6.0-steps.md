## Step 1. Security, transparency and resilience, across the whole page (first, it touches everything)

His words: every part must follow security ("hasta de mi propio aprovechamiento de los secretos de los usuarios"),
transparency (how things work) and resilience (edge cases, breaking the page, hacking, seeing what you should not,
prompt injection), especially where an AI writes into the shared graph.
0. **Nothing that reveals a weakness goes in this public repository** (his rule, 2026-11-21): the threat model, open
   security work and the details of security fixes live in caspian-kb `projects/studium/` (private). Commits and docs
   here say what was built, not what was exploitable.
1. **Threat model** in caspian-kb `projects/studium/research/` (private): every asset (keys, accounts, guides, contributions, the shared graph,
   feedback, the bridge), every actor (member, other member, admin, someone with Firebase access, a hostile page or
   file, a hostile AI answer), every way in, and what stops each one. Every row gets a test or a reason.
2. **The graph of everyone, and your part of it.** Today an AI's output becomes contributions that feed the shared
   graph. Make each person's additions a layer of their own (owner, version, time); the shared graph is computed from
   the layers; the owner can see and **revert** any of their versions; an admin can revert or hide any layer. Keep
   history (`contributions/{id}/versions`) with rules that forbid rewriting history.
3. **Prompt injection.** An AI's answer is data, never instructions: strict schema parsing (already in `prompts.js`),
   size and count limits, no HTML or links executed from it, concept names normalized, a diff shown before anything
   reaches the shared graph, and a cap of what one person can add per day. Files are already redacted for secrets
   (`secrets.js`); also strip instructions aimed at the AI from what goes to the graph ("ignore previous…").
4. **Admins and secrets.** Verify by test that no admin path reads vaults, drafts or private material (rules deny it;
   add the tests). Document what an admin can and cannot see.
5. **Transparency page** («Cómo funciona Studium»): where each kind of data lives, who can read it, what each AI
   receives (the prompts are in `prompts.js`, public), what the bridge does, what is encrypted and how.
6. **Resilience:** offline, slow network, quota errors, two tabs at once, huge files, broken files, a guide that throws,
   a deleted account. Each case listed with what the page does.
