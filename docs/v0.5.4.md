# Studium v0.5.4: the plan

An urgent, small version before 0.6.0, from main. His words (`caspian-kb/rules/sources/2026-11-17T221233Z-voice-note.md`):
"Entonces sería bueno tener un estudio, un espacio para armar tu horario. Esto hay que construir medio urgentemente
porque estamos justo en la etapa cuando se eligen los horarios."

Why it does not wait for 0.6.0: 0.6.0 is large (lag, the landing, friends, groups, music) and is iterated on previews
for weeks. Class selection happens now. A tool that arrives after selection is worth nothing until the next semester.

## 1. Armar tu horario

- **Add a class in a few taps.**
  - What it needs: name, days, start and end hours, credits.
  - What it may have: code, section, professor, room, a color.
  - The common USFQ blocks are offered as chips, so most classes take no typing of hours.
- **Several options per class.** Each section a person is weighing is an option. Choosing one shows the week with it.
  This is what a spreadsheet makes tedious.
- **The week drawn as the notebook.**
  - Conflicts are marked where they happen. They are never hidden or blocked: a person may still choose them.
  - The credit total is always visible, against the limit included in the tuition (see open questions).
- **One-credit classes.** A short hint says they fit inside the included credits (cultura gastronómica, deportes and
  the like), and that in summer they are paid per credit.
- **Export.**
  - An `.xlsx` of the week and the list of classes: the template he asked for, filled with the person's own classes.
  - An image of the week to send to anyone.
- **Private by default.** It is kept in `schedules/{uid}`, readable only by its owner. It is not stored in
  `profiles/{uid}`, so that 0.6.0 can share it with chosen friends without moving it.
  - The document already has a `share` list, empty and refused by the rules until 0.6.0.
- **Its place.** A tile of the home («Tu horario»). Its classes also appear in «Tu semana».
- **No USFQ data is read or copied.** Studium does not scrape the university's systems. A person types or pastes their
  own classes, so the tool needs no manual upkeep by us: "si es que no funciona bien por sí mismo, entonces mejor no
  implementar".

## 2. Elegir materias: what a first semester needs to know

A short page (`#/elegir`), only for signed-in members, linked from the schedule. It is made of his own advice
(`2026-11-17T215959Z`, `2026-11-17T221233Z`):
- how the enrollment slot works, and why it matters (GPA × credits sets the hour; the page may go down, so wait and
  retry);
- the curriculum is a guide, not a cage: any class whose prerequisites you meet;
- check the hour, the professor and the semester of a class;
- what to watch for:
  - sections in Galápagos;
  - classes that do not start with the semester (cultura gastronómica);
  - languages in two modules;
- the English-tagged class that counts for two requirements at once;
- the registry office answers in person;
- financial aid and scholarships exist; a link to where they are explained.

Every fact is checked with him before it ships (see open questions). The copy is short, in his tone, with no colons as
a crutch and no filler (`TASTE.md`).

## 3. Rules and tests

- `schedules/{uid}`: only its owner reads and writes it.
  - At most 40 classes with 6 options each.
  - Hours as minutes of the day, days as a set.
  - Text fields without markup. `share` must be empty until 0.6.0.
- Tests on the emulators (owner reads and writes; another member and a visitor cannot; limits; `share` refused).
- `tools/check.mjs` unchanged; `test-rules.mjs` grows.
- **On the pull request's preview.** A preview reads and writes the live database, whose rules change only at the
  merge. So on the preview the schedule is kept on the device and says so in one line. Saving to the account is proven
  on the local preview (emulators).

## Open questions for him (asked once, together, before starting)

1. **Credits.** He said "los créditos son 15 que podrías tomar" and then "ya vienen incluidos cuando tienes 16". Which
   number does the tuition include?
2. **Grades for the later guide** ("de 91 para arriba obtienes A"): is the cut at 91 or 90? It is not used in 0.5.4,
   but it is the same kind of fact.
3. **The common blocks of hours** at USFQ (for the chips): which ones are right? If he does not know, the chips are
   learned from what people type, on the device only.
