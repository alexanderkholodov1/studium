## 2. Armar tu horario, and «Elegir materias»

The first part after the lag is measured. It is built and shown on the preview first, and released with the rest.

**Getting your classes in, from whatever a person already has:**
- **Any format, any medium.** Everyone keeps their schedule their own way: the university's page, a screenshot, a photo
  of a paper, an Excel or a CSV in any layout, a PDF, text copied from anywhere. Studium does not ask for a template.
  - `files.js` reads the file in the browser, as it reads any file.
  - The person's own AI (the ways of `ai.js`, the chat included) turns it into Studium's format.
  - The person checks the result before it is saved.
- **No format is privileged.** His own sheet is personal ("es mi formato personal, no es algo que se usa por alguien
  mas"). It is one test case among many, never the model others must follow. The tests cover several kinds:
  - a screenshot of the university's own schedule;
  - a phone photo;
  - spreadsheets of different layouts (his among them);
  - plain pasted text.
- **By hand, in a few taps.** Name, days, block, credits. Code, NRC, professor and room are optional.

**What it does:**
- **Options.** Each alternative schedule (A, B, …) is an option, and switching between them to compare takes one tap.
- **The blocks of USFQ.** Classes start every hour and a half from 7:00, and each ends ten minutes before the next
  begins (7:00–8:20, 8:30–9:50, …). The blocks are offered as chips, so most classes take no typing of hours.
  - Many people write whole blocks (7:00–8:30) for ease. An import reads either form and stores the real hours.
  - The exact list is checked against the university's own published rules before it ships. The *Manual del Estudiante
    de Grado* is where USFQ says it describes the schedule.
- **Conflicts** are marked where they happen. They are never hidden or blocked: a person may still choose them.
- **Credits.** The total is always visible. What one might do with it is a tip (below), never an instruction of the
  tool.
- **Rooms read in words.** A room code becomes its building and floor, on hover: the letters for the buildings, the
  first digit for the floor. The table of buildings is checked before it ships; it is shared with the guide (§10).
- **Export.** An `.xlsx` of the week and the list of classes (the template he asked for, already filled) and an image
  of the week.
- **Private by default.** It is kept in `schedules/{uid}`, readable only by its owner, not in `profiles/{uid}`, so that
  §6 can share it with chosen friends without moving it.
- **Its place.** A tile of the home («Tu horario»). Its classes also appear in «Tu semana».

**The curriculum of each career («malla»):**
- The tool can show which classes of your malla you still need, and their prerequisites.
- **Students keep the mallas up to date themselves.** A member uploads their career's malla, a PDF or the copied
  page. Their AI reads it into a standard format (code, name, credits, semester, prerequisites, the general
  requirements). It is published like a contribution, with its source and date, and the newest one replaces the
  older.
- **The first one is Computer Science.** His files (`private/Materias/`, never in this public repository) are the test.
- **The autonomous source works** (built 2026-11-19). The university publishes every undergraduate malla on a public
  page by career code (`wsexternal.usfq.edu.ec/MallaCurricular-USFQ/...?codigoCarrera=1CMP`), with each class's
  prerequisites (alternatives and minimum grade) and the career's general requirements. `tools/malla.mjs` (an admin
  runs it; one request at a time, with a pause) writes them to `public/data/mallas/`, public data shipped with the
  app. The page is never read from members' browsers. Uploads by students stay as the way for what the page lacks.
- **«Mi malla»** (`#/malla`, from Mi horario and the profile menu): the career by semesters; the person marks what they
  passed (a whole semester in one tap), and sees what they can take now, what each class asks for and opens, and the
  career's requirements, with the date it was read. Career and passed classes live in the profile (`malla`,
  `passed`). Adding a class to a draft whose prerequisites are not marked asks, once, whether to keep it: a question,
  never a block.

**«Elegir materias»** (`#/elegir`, only for signed-in members): the student guide's page for choosing classes.
- **Tips where they help, not only on a page.** Short, single tips appear in context across Studium: next to the credit
  total, when a class is added, on the home, near the professors. Each is one or two friendly lines and leads to the
  guide for the whole story. They follow the rules of the hints of today (`hints.js`): few, never obvious, never in the
  way, and gone once read.
- **Advice, never orders.** A tip says what to consider and leaves the choice to the student, because Studium is not
  responsible for what students decide. His example, on 2026-11-19:
  - Yes: "considera que en el semestre suele sobrar un crédito, que puedes aprovechar si quieres (igual lo estás
    pagando) con materias de 1 crédito de tu malla".
  - No: "toma Deportes aquí".
  - The same holds for every tip: "considera", "puede convenirte", "muchos lo hacen así", never "debes" or "toma".
  - Where a rule is the university's (a requirement, a deadline), the tip says it is the university's rule and cites
    it.
- **Its sources:** his advice (`2026-11-17T215959Z`, `2026-11-17T221233Z`), the malla, and above all the *Manual del
  Estudiante de Grado*.
- **The manual gets a session of its own**, read in order, before the tips are written. It holds many rules a student
  should know and rarely hears, such as this one he learned: from a certain cohort on, repeating a class rules out Summa
  Cum Laude and other honors.
- **The tips:**
  - **The enrollment slot.** How it works: GPA times credits sets your hour. Why it matters. The page may go down: wait
    and retry.
  - **The malla is a guide, not a cage.** You can consider any class whose prerequisites you meet, and the registry
    office answers in person.
  - **Check each class:** the hour, the professor and the semester. Watch for:
    - sections in Galápagos;
    - classes that do not start with the semester (Cultura Gastronómica);
    - languages in two modules.
  - **Requirements that combine.** A course taught in English (code ending in E) can also be a general-education class.
    It meets two requirements at once.
  - **Requirements easy to miss**, from the malla itself: Writing Intensive (after all English levels), Ser Dragón,
    PASEC, PASEM, Deportes, Cultura Gastronómica (from the second semester), and the minimum of C in the classes of the
    college of Sciences and Engineering.
  - **Credits.** The tuition includes 16, and the usual load is 15 (five classes of three). So one credit is often
    left over, already paid for. You might use it, if you want, for a one-credit class of your malla. Outside the
    semester such classes are paid per credit.
  - **Everything else the manual says a student must consider:** honors and repeating classes, withdrawals, grades and
    the kardex, and more. Each tip cites the section of the manual it comes from, and its edition.
  - **Help with paying:** financial aid and scholarships exist, with a link to where they are explained.

**Rules and tests:**
- `schedules/{uid}`: only its owner, at most 6 options of 40 classes. `share` is empty until §6.
- The profile's `malla` (a career code) and `passed` (at most 150 classes). The mallas themselves are static files.
- On the PR preview, before the merge publishes the rules, the schedule is kept on the device and says so in one line.

**Done means:**
- Schedules in several formats import correctly, with real hours. The formats include a screenshot of the
  university's page, a photo, spreadsheets of different layouts and pasted text.
- A first-semester schedule made by hand takes under two minutes.
- The `.xlsx` opens in Excel and Google Sheets; the image reads well on a phone.

## 4. Friends, and the people you see

- **A friend is added with a link or a code.**
  - The code is long and random, and the person can make a new one, which voids the old one.
  - Friendship needs both sides: the link proposes it, and the other person accepts it.
  - Either side ends it alone.
- **Your name for friends.** Each person chooses the name their friends see; `nickname` in the profile today is reused.
  Nobody else sees it, and nothing they contribute shows it.
- **Preset avatars.**
  - A set drawn for Studium, in the notebook style, with depth.
  - They replace the six colors of today's `avatar` field, which become their backgrounds.
  - No uploaded pictures: they cost storage and need moderation. Uploads and a Duolingo-like builder are in the roadmap.
- **Only members of the same community** can be friends, as with everything else in Studium.

## 5. Study groups: by invitation, without chat (his choice)

- **What a group is.**
  - A room with a code: the same kind of code as friends, renewable by its creator.
  - The creator can remove a member.
- **What it has.**
  - A shared focus timer, the same one of the home.
  - Shared music (below).
  - Who is studying now.
  - Fixed, positive reactions.
- **There is no free text in a group** (his choice: "sin chat"). Free text exists only where it has a purpose and a
  reader: a professor's post-it (§8) and a message to the admins (§9).
- **"Who is studying now"** follows the rule of `presence/{uid}`: it is written on real events (joining, starting the
  timer, leaving), never on a timer.
- **Reads stay in the free plan.**
  - A group is one document plus one per member, listened to only while the group is open.
  - The budget is written in the pull request: reads for a group of six over a two-hour session.

## 7. Music: YouTube in full, Spotify embedded (his choice)

- **YouTube.**
  - A player inside Studium, with its own controls, on the YouTube IFrame Player API (no key is needed).
  - It plays YouTube and YouTube Music links, songs and playlists, pasted by the person. No search: search needs the
    Data API and its daily quota.
  - In a group it plays in sync for everyone: one person leads, and the rest follow play, pause, seek and the track.
  - The browser allows sound only after a tap. A follower presses «Escuchar con el grupo» once.
  - A video whose owner forbids embedding says so in words and offers to open it on YouTube.
- **Spotify.**
  - Its embedded player, for the playlist, album or track the person pastes.
  - Full tracks when the person is signed in to Spotify in that browser, previews otherwise. This is checked on the
    preview with a free account and a Premium one before it is written on the page.
  - Not synchronized in groups.
- **Why not more.** Spotify's Web API admits only a few hand-approved users for a small app, and its Jams have no
  public API. So "compartir jams de Spotify" is honestly not possible from Studium. The group shares YouTube instead.

## 8. Professors: post-its, and what to expect from each

His words (`2026-11-16T220707Z`): more memorable, more complete, more attractive. The purpose, as he put it on
2026-11-19: "que los chicos tomen los profesores correctos, sabiendo lo que esperar de cada uno".

**Positivity is a rule between members, not about professors.** It stays as it is for what members do with each other
(thanks for a guide, groups, reactions). An opinion of a professor's teaching may be critical, including "no lo
recomiendo", because that is exactly what helps another student choose. The README is corrected to say this.

**What a person writes:**
- **The quick way.** Recommend or not, in one tap. Then the fixed tags of today, optional.
- **The deep way, if they want it.** A few guiding questions, all optional:
  - how they grade;
  - how good the syllabus is;
  - how much they help when you need something;
  - how flexible they are with grades;
  - how much they forgive by the end of the semester;
  - free text for whatever fits no category ("está prohibiendo salir de clase").
- **Tied to a course and a semester**, because "el comportamiento de los profesores suele cambiar de semestre en
  semestre".
- **Anonymous by default.** A person may sign with their name. The author is known only to the rules and the admins,
  so that abuse can be stopped.

**What a person sees:**
- **Post-its on the professors page**, colored by recommendation. They lift and move on hover, and open to be read.
- **The summary.** For each professor, and each course with them: whether to take them, what to consider, what changed
  recently. Short and clear, so nobody has to read every post-it, though they can.
  - It is made by an admin's AI from the panel, on demand, and kept with its date. Nothing runs on a timer, and no
    visitor spends AI on it.
  - Any member may also ask their own AI about a professor, with the post-its as context.

**Keeping it about teaching:**
- A post-it is about the teaching, not the person. The guiding questions steer it there, and one line says so where
  the person writes.
- Any member reports a post-it with one tap. Past a few reports it is hidden until an admin reviews it.
- Free text has a size limit, no links, and a rate limit per account.

**Rules:** `notes/{id}` (a professor's post-its) is written by students, with a structure the rules check, and read by
members. `summaries/{prof}` is written by admins. `reports/{id}` is written by members and read by admins.

## 9. Feedback, proposals and «Escríbeme»

- **Anyone signed in writes.** A member sends a comment, reports an error or proposes a feature.
  - Only the admins and its sender read it.
  - Its fields: kind (error, idea, comment, "porque sí"), the text, and the page it came from, with the version and the
    browser for an error.
  - No screenshots. They would need file storage, which Studium does not have; a link to one is accepted in the text.
- **The admins answer** from the admin panel, and the sender sees the answer in «Escríbeme».
- **A summary on demand.** In the panel, the admin's own AI (the ways of `ai.js`) can summarize what arrived and group
  repeated errors. It runs on demand only, as everything in the panel: never on a timer.
- **The contact section** (`2026-11-18T192833Z`), in his tone, half playful, half rebellious:
  - why to write: an error, an idea, a suggestion;
  - and a reason that is not formal: "o escríbeme porque sí".
  - The joke about being single, which he asked to keep on record: proposed copy is shown to him on the preview.
    Nothing personal is published without his yes.
  - It is only for signed-in members, as the rest of what is about him beyond the landing.
- **Rules:** `feedback/{id}` is written by its sender. It is read by its sender and the admins, answered only by the
  admins. It has limits of size and of rate (a few per day per account).
